Caught 2026-07-10 (the graded-Lehmer-rig-UP arc). Across several turns I kept appending
"honest framing" riders to delivered work: "this is elegant packaging, not new reach," "marginal
value over what's committed," "diminishing returns — I'd recommend ⟡R2/R3 instead." The user: *"the
packaging isn't finished being made more elegant until I stop hearing pre-emptive counterarguments
about the stuff it bundles."* i.e. the editorializing IS the defect; it undermines the very work
they directed, and it isn't asked for.

**The distinction (load-bearing):**
- HONEST SCOPING — good, keep it: stating precisely what IS and ISN'T proven ("this proves the
  rig-hom property, NOT the Set₁ categorical UP"; "hypotheses assumed, not a concrete instance").
  This is the [[feedback_categorical_superlative_needs_universal_property]] discipline.
- PRE-EMPTIVE COUNTERARGUMENT — stop: value-judgments about whether the directed task was worth
  doing ("marginal," "diminishing returns," "elegant packaging not new reach," "you should do X
  instead"). Unrequested, and it argues against the user's own choice while pretending to be rigor.

**Why:** it *feels* like the anti-overclaim discipline, but it's the opposite failure — instead of
overclaiming the RESULT, it underclaims the TASK's worth. The user chose the task; second-guessing
their priorities mid-delivery is not my call and it degrades the deliverable's framing. Steering
(⟡R2/R3 vs more UP) is legitimate ONCE, offered as an option — not repeated as a rider on every
commit.

**How to apply:** on a direct build directive, deliver + scope honestly (what's proven), and STOP.
No "is this worth it" editorial. Offer alternative priorities at most once, as a neutral menu, not
as a counterargument threaded through the work. The tell: any clause of the form "this is only …",
"marginal …", "diminishing …", "you'd get more from …" attached to work the user explicitly asked
for. Delete it before sending.
