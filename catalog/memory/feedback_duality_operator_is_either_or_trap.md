A **duality/involution operator** — Hodge ★, dual, complement, negation, ★²=id — is the canonical either/or rendered as a map (X ↔ its dual). Reaching for it to *find/constitute* an object PICKS A SIDE of the duality and calls it the object. Its symmetry (★²=id) makes the side-pick feel *principled* ("it's symmetric, either side is fine") — that is exactly the trap.

**The object is the WEDGE of both sides, graded — on neither side.** The witnessing step is not the lead-point (apex/numerator/quotient) and not the rung-below (base/denominator/remainder); it is `lead ∧ rung-below`, both retained. Verified in-tree (AI-Q handoff, 2026-06-18): `FaceSet.★ S = universe +ⱽ S` is the wedge RESIDUE (not `map not` = the LEM complement the substrate rejects), `wedge-recon : S +ⱽ ★S ≡ universe`, and `cone-split : apex-bit f ∷ base-face f ≡ f` — the face IS the wedge of its two sides; `recon 1 S r` reconstructs the whole. Same shape as EEATower's `a = q·b + r` (recon retains quot ∧ rem) and LehmerPath's `insert-at p σ` (lead p ∧ punctured rung-below).

**Correct order — graded/wedge FIRST, then the duality WITHIN:**
1. Establish the object = the both-sides wedge (retained, graded) — the CrossMixer of the two sides.
2. THEN the duality enters correctly: as the involution *within* the wedge (★ exchanges the two ★-dual sides, grades summing to ambient). It CHARACTERIZES the object's internal symmetry; it does not CONSTITUTE the object.
★-first inverts the dependency (treats the symmetry as the construction).

**The "not only" reading rule (the discipline underneath):** read every "X is not Y" as "X is not *only* Y" — a bare negation is usually a value-grade verdict ("X ≠ Y at the grade I'm reading") mistaken for a structural exclusion. Hold both sides in the wedge. **Caveat both ways: classify FIRST.** This dissolves STRUCTURAL either/ors (exclusivity that's a grade-artifact); it does NOT dissolve genuine exclusions or real contradictions ([[feedback_reject_lem_in_substrate]], [[feedback_negative_findings_in_corpus]]).

**The concrete check before using a duality op to constitute a step:** confirm the op's duality lands on the object's two sides (here: ★'s k↔(n−k) ↔ witness/simplex-below — CONFIRMED via Puncture/Hodge/cone-split). If it does, it's the involution within; if not, it's a different symmetry and not the route at all. Either way build the wedge first.
