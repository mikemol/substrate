A "good enough for the moment" approximation is NOT saved time — it is structural SCAR TISSUE. A shitty
approximation imposes obstructions that make clean, elegant landings much harder as the orders get
higher and higher; that first "good enough" gets stuck with you, and scar tissue grows all around it.
Kept clean/mature instead, you can fold as many mature and fruitful things into a TIGHT KERNEL as you
want, and get ever more power and expressivity for it.

This CORRECTS the "over-grew" framing I used for the kernel-perf hardware-modeling arc (do not repeat
it): it did NOT over-grow. It was allowed to grow up to be FRUITFUL rather than tech debt — grown to
maturity so it folds cleanly into the JEA dispatcher (it became the control-law oracle), instead of a
quick "just enough for the dispatcher" approximation that would have become an obstruction. Thoroughness
toward a clean, exact, mature artifact is the right call, not bloat.

This IS the substrate ethos, named: exact; escalate-don't-truncate/wrap; no hidden quotient; never
discard residue ([[feedback_never_discard_residue]]); the Agda-on-GPU charter's escalate-don't-wrap.
Folding mature pieces into a kernel is the whole game (Free⊣Forgetful term-algebra construction).

**How to apply:** when tempted by "good enough for now / a quick approximation / a stub we'll fix
later," build the MATURE, exact version instead. The cost is front-loaded ONCE; the approximation's
cost compounds forever (scar tissue at every higher order). Judge thoroughness by "does this fold
cleanly into the kernel later?" not by "is it minimal right now." Don't apologize for growing a thing
to maturity — that's the point.
