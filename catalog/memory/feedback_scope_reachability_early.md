When an arc turns out much larger than its label suggested (a "◆tighten (MED)" that the reuse-search reveals
needs two hard combinatorial cores from scratch), do NOT explore for ~15 turns and then oscillate between
"grind it" and "should I checkpoint?". That meta-indecision — the WAFFLE — itself consumes turns and reads as
reluctance without delivering either the build or a clean decision.

**The fix — apply the realizability REACHABILITY gate EARLY.** As soon as the reuse-search shows the true shape
(constructible ✓, but reachable = a dedicated multi-hour session), state that honestly: the proven-so-far
milestone, the precise remaining hard pieces, and the cost. Surface it ONCE (an AskUserQuestion or a crisp
status), then either build (if directed) or bank the green residue + a complete scaffold. The user prizes
realizability honesty (the four gates: constructible, reachable, observable, coverable) — an early, accurate
reachability report is more useful than fifteen turns of exploration followed by hedging.

**The tell:** repeatedly almost-asking "want me to continue?" without either committing to the build or giving a
clean scoped decision — construct-dont-classify's poll-don't-build in reverse. When you notice it, stop: name the
reachable milestone, name the cost, surface once.

**Origin (2026-07-19).** On ◆tighten-insertion-parity I explored the sign/Fin/Coxeter machinery for ~15 turns,
then waffled for several more between grinding and checkpointing. The honest move was available early: the
reuse-search had already shown BOTH routes need a hard core (perm surjectivity / the id-perm Coxeter count) with
no s-recovers-style shortcut — a genuine multi-hour arc. Scoping that at turn 5, not turn 20, was the discipline.
