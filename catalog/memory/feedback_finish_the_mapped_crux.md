Recurring this arc (Ⓕ.spectral, ≈3×): I do the easy reuse + infra, commit a gated checkpoint, and **defer the single hard lemma to "next turn"** — orbit-sum deferred, then the connector deferred (even once mapped to 5 steps where only `orbit-hits-once` is new). Committing gated increments is the repo's discipline and good; the FAULT is stopping at the crux when the crux is already mapped and bounded. It has the texture of diligence (a clean green commit) while actually being the wall/poll reflex one notch in — "the hard part is hard, I'll land it later."

**Why:** a mapped ≤1–2-lemma crux is no longer open-ended; deferring it is a choice to not land, and it accretes (each turn re-pays context + a full-build). The user reads repeated "checkpoint + here's the precise remaining" as not-finishing. Sibling of [[feedback_declaring_a_wall_is_the_rebuild_reflex]] (wall) and construct-dont-classify #4 poll-don't-build (stop-and-ask) — this is stop-and-defer.

**How to apply:** before ending a turn on "committed the infra; the remaining is steps 1–N," ask *is the remainder mapped to ≤1–2 genuinely-new lemmas?* If yes, **push through and land it** (write the lemma, compile, close), don't checkpoint. Checkpoint only when the remainder is genuinely large/open or a natural multi-session boundary. The bias for a mapped small crux is to finish, not to defer. (Distinguish: committing because the build is green AND more real work remains = good; committing to avoid the one hard lemma = the fault.)
